# Cover image — 16:9, PNG or JPG, required for submission

The submission gallery is a wall of thumbnails. At 200px wide a screenshot turns to mush and a
gradient looks like every other generated cover. **Typography wins at thumbnail size**, so the
image's job is to leave room for a wordmark, not to be busy.

The palette is **deep navy and signal yellow**, matching the deck
([`docs/DISCIPLINED-AI-REAL-MARKETS-BRIGHTER-OUTCOMES.pptx`](../docs/DISCIPLINED-AI-REAL-MARKETS-BRIGHTER-OUTCOMES.pptx))
so cover, deck and video end card read as one project.

**One caveat, stated rather than buried:** several teams in this track named themselves some
variant of Aegis, and their covers all look like security software — shields, locks, dark blue.
Navy is now the crowded half of that gallery. What separates this cover is the **yellow** and
the editorial restraint: no shield, no lock, no glow, no circuitry, enormous empty space. If a
prompt below starts drifting toward "cybersecurity product", it has failed and the negatives
need tightening. (The dashboard itself is warm espresso and cream; the cover follows the deck,
not the site.)

The one idea to carry: **the model may decline a trade. It can never invent one.**

## Prompt A — the audit trail *(recommended)*

Paste as one block. Every colour is named by hex because image models drift toward teal
otherwise.

> A 16:9 minimalist editorial poster, flat vector-print quality. Deep navy background, a soft
> radial field from #0E2C74 at the centre-left falling to #06122F at the edges, with a very
> subtle fine grid barely visible in the darkness. Vast empty space. Along the left third, a
> faint vertical column of dim monospace log lines suggesting an audit trail — mostly
> illegible, deliberately low contrast, drawn in muted steel blue — with one single line picked
> out in bright signal yellow #FFC61A. A thin yellow #FFC61A horizontal rule sits in the lower
> third, spanning about a quarter of the width. Along the very bottom edge, a narrow band of
> flat-topped jagged yellow shapes, like a low skyline, no more than one twentieth of the image
> height. No charts, no candlesticks, no robots, no glowing brains, no circuit-board motifs, no
> shields, no locks, no lens flare, no neon glow, no stock photography, no people, no text
> anywhere. High contrast, print-like, editorial restraint. Leave the centre-right two thirds
> completely empty for typography to be added afterwards.

Then set the type yourself, in the empty right two thirds:

```
BRIGHTLINE                            large · mono or a tight grotesque · #FAFAFA
a bright-line rule admits no          small · #FFC61A · sentence case
judgment. neither does ours.
```

## Prompt B — the one-item menu

Dramatises the rulebook: one permitted choice, and the option to decline.

> A 16:9 poster photographed from directly above: a plain pale card resting on a deep navy
> matte surface in soft directional light, with a single thin yellow #FFC61A rule running along
> the lower edge of the frame. The card carries one short line of elegant serif text and
> nothing else — enormous empty space around it. Muted, editorial, film-still quality, shallow
> depth of field, cool shadows, one warm yellow highlight. No people, no hands, no logos, no
> charts, no shields, no glow, and no text anywhere except that one line on the card.

## Prompt C — the pinned dial

The graveyard idea: a gauge reading maximum because it has only ever been read once.

> A 16:9 editorial poster: a single analogue gauge, needle pinned hard at maximum, isolated on
> a deep navy #06122F background with dramatic side lighting. Brass, yellow enamel and glass —
> a scientific instrument, slightly worn — the needle in bright yellow #FFC61A. Minimal, moody,
> high contrast, enormous empty space around it. No text on the dial face, no charts, no
> digital UI, no shields, no circuitry, no people.

## Practical

**Generate the composition, then set the text yourself in Canva or Figma.** Image models
mangle typography — you will get `BRIGHTUNE` or `BRIGHTLIME`, and the wordmark is the first
thing a judge sees. Ask for the space to be left empty, then set the type properly.

- Export at **1920×1080**, PNG or JPG
- **Test it at 200px wide.** If the wordmark is not instantly readable, make it bigger
- Yellow is the accent, not the ground. If it covers more than about a tenth of the frame the
  cover stops reading as editorial and starts reading as a warning sign
- The same wordmark goes on the deck title slide, the video end card, and the naming post
- Palette, matching the deck exactly:

| | |
|---|---|
| royal navy | `#0E2C74` — the field, centre-left |
| deep navy | `#06122F` — the ground, falling off to the edges |
| signal yellow | `#FFC61A` — the one highlighted line, rules, the jagged bottom band |
| deep gold | `#C99400` — the shadowed second row of the jagged band |
| off-white | `#FAFAFA` — the wordmark |
